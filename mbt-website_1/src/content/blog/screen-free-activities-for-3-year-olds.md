The best screen-free activities for a three year old are short, hands-on and open-ended, using things you already have at home. At this age a child plays close to you, likes a bit of repetition, and stays longest with an activity they can change as they go. You do not need a craft cupboard or a plan. You need ten minutes and whatever is in reach.

Here are activities that hold a three year old's attention, sorted by the mood they are in, plus a few ways to make each one last longer.

## What actually holds a 3 year old's attention

A three year old can usually stay with one thing for a few minutes at a time, sometimes much longer when it is their idea. The trick is to follow their lead rather than run an activity at them. Offer a simple starting point, then let them take it somewhere. The activities below are built that way, with a clear start and plenty of room to wander.

## When they need to move

A three year old who is climbing the walls needs their body busy before anything calm will work.

- **Animal walks.** Hop like a frog, waddle like a penguin, stomp like an elephant from one end of the room to the other.
- **Freeze dance.** Hum a tune, stop suddenly, everyone freezes. Silly, active, and quietly great for self-control.
- **The cushion course.** Lay cushions across the floor as stepping stones and cross the "river" together.
- **Fast and slow.** Move like a snail, then like a cheetah, and talk about the difference.

## When they need to settle

- **Water play, with you right there.** A bowl of water, a few cups, a towel underneath. Pouring and filling is endlessly absorbing. Never leave a small child alone with water, even a little.
- **Posting games.** Cut a slot in a box lid and let them post lids, cards or pegs through it. Simple, and strangely satisfying for a three year old.
- **Sorting the odds and ends.** Buttons, pegs, socks, cutlery. Sorting by colour or size is early maths, and small children love a proper job.
- **Playdough at the table.** Squishing, rolling and poking builds the little hand muscles that later hold a pencil.

## Out in the backyard

- **Make a bird bath.** A shallow dish, a few stones so small birds can stand, and fresh water. Set it out and watch who visits. We give birds water, never food.
- **A stick and a patch of dirt.** Draw, dig, build. It is the oldest toy there is, and one of the best.
- **A slow bug hunt.** Turn over a pot or a leaf and see who lives underneath. Wonder out loud together.
- **Chalk on the path.** Draw around their shadow, then check again in an hour and see how it has moved.

## How to make an activity last longer

The activity matters less than how you hold it. A few small things stretch ten minutes into thirty:

- **Follow their lead.** If they turn the bird bath into a swimming pool for a toy dinosaur, go with it.
- **Narrate, don't direct.** "You are stacking them so carefully" keeps them going far better than "do it like this."
- **Add one small change.** When interest dips, add a jug, a funnel, a second colour. One new element buys another burst.
- **Let the mess happen.** The spills and the crumbs are part of the learning. Tidy after, not during.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long can a 3 year old focus on one activity?**
Usually a few minutes when it is directed by you, and much longer when it is their own idea. Expect to rotate through two or three short activities rather than one long one.

**What are good screen-free activities for a 3 year old indoors?**
Water play with supervision, posting games, sorting odds and ends, playdough, cushion courses and animal walks all work indoors with things already in the house.

**How do I get my 3 year old off a screen without a meltdown?**
Give the change some warning, then offer a hands-on activity to move toward rather than just switching the screen off. Our [witching-hour resets](/blog/beating-the-witching-hour/) work well for tricky transitions.
