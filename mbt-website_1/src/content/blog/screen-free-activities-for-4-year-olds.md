The best screen-free activities for a four year old lean into what is new at this age: bigger pretend play with a storyline, simple games with a few rules, and making things with their own hands. A four year old can stay with something longer than a toddler, play alongside other children and follow a couple of steps, so you can offer a little more and step back a little more. You do not need a plan or a craft cupboard. You need ten minutes and whatever is already in the house.

Here are activities that suit where a four year old is up to, grouped by the kind of play, with the research on why each one earns its place.

## What is different about play at four

A four year old is not just a bigger three year old. Australia's [Raising Children Network notes that around four to five](https://raisingchildren.net.au/preschoolers/development/development-tracker/4-5-years) a child loves make-believe play, is learning to get along with others, cooperates, "says sorry and agrees to rules," and can "cut with child-safe scissors" and "do up buttons and write their first name and some letters."

That shift, towards longer stories, more cooperating and steadier hands, changes what holds their attention. The activities below are built around it, so they land better than something pitched at a toddler.

## Big pretend play

At four, make-believe grows a plot. Children this age love trying on roles, from running a shop to being a doctor. Give them a scenario and a prop or two, then let them take charge of the story.

- **Set up a shop.** A few tins from the pantry, some paper money, a bag. They price things, you buy them, then you swap. Counting and talking, built in.
- **A doctor or vet clinic.** A teddy patient, a tea towel for a bandage, a notepad for the "notes." Looking after someone is big, satisfying play at this age.
- **Restaurant.** They take your order, "cook" it, bring it over. Add a menu they draw themselves and you have another ten minutes.
- **A small-world box.** A shoebox, some toy animals or little figures, a few bits of fabric for grass and water. A whole world they control.

## Games with a few simple rules

Four is the age when taking turns and agreeing on rules starts to click, which Raising Children Network lists as part of normal development now. A simple game is a gentle way to practise waiting, losing and trying again.

- **Matching and memory games.** Turn cards face down, take turns finding pairs. Easy to make at home with two of anything.
- **A treasure hunt.** Hide five objects, give a clue for each. They love the searching, and clues stretch their thinking.
- **Skittles.** Line up empty bottles, roll a ball, count what falls. Movement and early maths together.
- **Follow the leader or Simon Says.** Great for listening, self-control and a good giggle.

## Making and building with their hands

A four year old's hands can do far more than a toddler's, so give them real jobs. This is also how the little muscles for writing get strong.

- **Cut and stick.** Child-safe scissors, old magazines, a glue stick. Cutting is tricky and brilliant for them, so let it be messy.
- **Threading.** Pasta tubes or beads on a shoelace. Fiddly, calming, and great for focus.
- **Build a tower or a den.** Blocks, cushions, a sheet over two chairs. Planning and problem solving, dressed up as fun.
- **Playdough models.** At four they will make actual things now, a snail, a nest, a person with arms and legs, not just squish it.

## Getting outside and moving

Four year olds need a surprising amount of movement to feel right. Australia's [24-hour movement guidelines](https://www.health.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/2021/05/24-hour-movement-guidelines-birth-to-5-years-fact-sheet.pdf) recommend children aged three to five get at least 180 minutes of physical activity across the day, and that at least 60 minutes of that is energetic play. The simplest way to reach it is to get outside.

- **An obstacle course.** Over the log, under the table, around the tree, jump the chalk line. Change it each time.
- **A balance challenge.** Walk a plank on the ground, or a chalk line, heel to toe. Harder than it looks, and they will want to master it.
- **A nature scavenger hunt.** Something smooth, something yellow, something smaller than your thumb. Looking closely is its own skill.
- **Target throwing.** A bucket and some rolled socks or soft balls. Step back a pace each time they get it in.

## Quieter activities that grow early maths and letters

By four, many children can count to ten, know some letters and numbers out of order, and understand opposites like high and low, as Raising Children Network describes. You grow those through play, not flashcards.

- **Sorting and counting.** Buttons, pegs, pasta. Sort by colour, then count each pile out loud.
- **A letter hunt.** Find the first letter of their name on signs, packets and books around the house.
- **The opposites game.** You say big, they say small. Fast, then slow. High, then low. Silly and sneakily clever.
- **Reading together.** Still the single best quiet activity there is. Here is [why reading aloud makes such a difference](/blog/does-reading-aloud-make-a-difference/).

## How to make an activity last, and when to step back

The activity matters less than how you hold it. A few small things stretch ten minutes into thirty.

- **Follow their lead.** If the shop becomes a spaceship, climb aboard.
- **Add one twist.** When interest dips, add a prop, a rule or a second player. One new thing buys another burst.
- **Let them do the hard bit.** The fumbling with scissors or the wobble on the beam is the learning. Sit on your hands and let them work it out. We wrote about why [the hard bit is the point](/blog/the-hard-bit-is-the-point/).
- **Keep screens in proportion.** The same guidelines suggest no more than one hour of sedentary screen time a day for this age, so a tap-and-go activity beats a tap-and-scroll one most afternoons.

## Why this matters more than it used to

Screen-free play at four is not about keeping a child busy. It grows the human strengths a screen cannot teach: attention, curiosity, real-world know-how and the nerve to have a go. Those are exactly the strengths that will matter most as our kids grow up alongside AI. ## Frequently asked questions

**What are good screen-free activities for a 4 year old indoors?**
Pretend-play setups like a shop or a vet clinic, matching and memory games, cutting and sticking, threading, building a den, playdough models and sorting games all work indoors with things already in the house.

**How long should a 4 year old be able to concentrate for?**
It varies a lot. A four year old usually stays with something you have set for a few minutes, and far longer when it is their own idea. Rotating through two or three short activities is normal, so do not worry if they jump about.

**How much physical activity does a 4 year old need each day?**
Australia's 24-hour movement guidelines recommend at least 180 minutes of physical activity a day for three to five year olds, including at least 60 minutes of energetic play. Outdoor play is the easiest way to get there.

**How do I get my 4 year old off a screen without a meltdown?**
Give them some warning first, then offer a hands-on activity to move towards rather than just switching the screen off. Having one picked and ready makes the swap far easier.
