Open-ended play, the kind with no instructions and no right answer, is some of the most valuable play a three to five year old can do. When there is no finished thing to copy, the child makes every decision themselves, and that is exactly where attention, curiosity and confidence grow. I was reminded of this in the best possible way on a rainy morning in Adelaide, and the happy surprise is that you can set up the same thing at home for almost nothing.

## The morning my daughter called "the best day"

We spent a couple of hours at [Doodah](https://doodahplay.com.au/), a little creative play studio on Elizabeth Street in Croydon, run by a mother and daughter, Maria and Rita. It is a stay-and-play space where children wander between a few simple stations at their own pace. A giant paint wall. A table of coloured rice. A making table stacked with bits and pieces. A pretend kitchen in the corner.

My nearly four year old painted, poured rice for the longest time, threaded beads, glued things she could not name, and somewhere near the end announced that this was "the best day." She was not wrong. But the part that stayed with me was not that she loved it. It was why she loved it, because once I saw it, I realised I could give her the same thing in our own kitchen any afternoon of the week.

## Why open-ended play works so well

Nothing at Doodah tells a child what to make. There is no template taped to the easel, no photo of the finished craft, no grown-up hovering to say do it like this. The space simply assumes children are capable, hands them good materials, and gets out of the way.

That is the whole secret. When a child decides what to do, how to do it, and when it is finished, their brain is doing the real work. They are planning, choosing, solving a small problem, changing their mind, having another go. A worksheet or a paint-by-numbers craft skips all of that and hands them someone else's decisions instead. Open-ended play is slower and messier and far more valuable, which is also why [play is how young children learn](/blog/why-play-is-how-young-children-learn/) in the first place, and why the strengths it builds are exactly [the human skills AI cannot easily replace](/blog/human-skills-ai-cant-replace/).

Here is what Doodah does beautifully, and how to do each one at home.

## Painting with no picture in mind

<figure>
  <img src="/blog/doodah-easel-wall-painting.jpg" alt="Two children in denim smocks painting standing up at a large wall marked Paint Me at Doodah creative play studio in Croydon, Adelaide" />
  <figcaption>The paint wall at Doodah. No picture to copy, just a brush, some colour and a big blank space.</figcaption>
</figure>

The paint wall says "Paint Me!" and that is the only instruction. Kids stand up, which little bodies love, and go wherever the brush takes them. No one asks what is it, no one suggests a sun in the corner.

At home you do not need a wall. Tape a big sheet of paper to the fence, the shower screen or the kitchen cupboards, give them one or two colours to start, and say nothing about what to paint. Standing up and painting big is half the joy. If you can only offer one thing, say "you choose the colours" and then genuinely let them. The less you steer, the more they bring.

## A sensory table you cannot get wrong

<figure>
  <img src="/blog/doodah-sensory-rice-table.jpg" alt="A young child scooping and pouring coloured rice with cups and funnels at a sensory table" />
  <figcaption>My daughter at the rice table, pouring and scooping. She stayed here far longer than I expected.</figcaption>
</figure>

A big tub of coloured rice, some cups, a funnel, a few plastic dinosaurs. That was it, and she poured and scooped and buried things for ages. Pouring is one of those quiet obsessions that never seems to run out, because there is no way to do it wrong.

Recreate it with a washing basket or a deep tray on a towel, filled with dried rice, pasta or lentils. Add scoops, cups and a funnel from the kitchen drawer. Colouring the rice is a two minute job with a splash of vinegar and a few drops of food colouring, left to dry on a tray. Sit close, let the spills happen, and tidy after rather than during. It is the same instinct behind good old [water play and other screen-free activities for little ones](/blog/screen-free-activities-for-3-year-olds/).

## Everything out, sorted, and in reach

<figure>
  <img src="/blog/doodah-craft-table-pencils.jpg" alt="A craft table at Doodah with coloured pencils sorted by colour in trays, pom poms, beads and children's paper creations" />
  <figcaption>Every colour sorted and in reach, so a child can just begin.</figcaption>
</figure>

The making table had pencils sorted by colour, pom poms in a jar, beads, ribbon, all of it out and reachable. A child does not have to ask, or wait, or find a grown-up to open something. They can just start, and the sorting itself is satisfying to little minds that love order.

You can copy this on a tea tray. A few jars or muffin tins with pencils, buttons, stickers and scraps of paper, sitting where your child can help themselves. Half the magic is simply that it is ready. When there is no barrier between the idea and the doing, they begin far more often, and they stick with it longer.

## Loose parts, the humble hero

<figure>
  <img src="/blog/doodah-craft-materials.jpg" alt="A shelf of open-ended craft materials at Doodah including paper straws, pipe cleaners, coloured paper, buttons and cardboard tubes" />
  <figcaption>Straws, pipe cleaners, buttons and paper, all out where little hands can help themselves.</figcaption>
</figure>

The shelves were full of what educators call loose parts, bits with no fixed purpose. Straws, pipe cleaners, cardboard tubes, coloured paper, corks, lids. A pipe cleaner can be a worm, a bracelet, a pair of glasses or a letter of the alphabet, and the child decides which.

This is the cheapest and most powerful setup of all, and most of it is already in your recycling. Keep a box of clean tubes, lids, boxes, corks, offcuts and scraps, add tape and glue, and let them build whatever is in their head that day. Loose parts are pure decision-making, which is why they feed curiosity so well. If you want more on protecting that spark, we wrote a whole piece on [how to raise a curious child](/blog/how-to-raise-a-curious-child/).

## You really do not need the studio

A place like Doodah is a treat, and worth it on a rainy day when the walls are closing in. You can [book a stay-and-play session](https://doodahplay.com.au/book/), have a look at the different stations on their [explore page](https://doodahplay.com.au/explore/), or follow [@doodahplay on Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/doodahplay/) for a peek inside. But the thing that made my daughter's morning was not the beautiful handmade space. It was open materials, a bit of freedom, and no one telling her what to make. All three of those are free, and they fit on your kitchen table.

You do not need a craft cupboard or a plan either. Ten unhurried minutes of child-led play does more than an hour of anything with instructions. ## Frequently asked questions

**What is open-ended play?**
It is play with no set outcome and no right answer, where the child decides what to do and when it is finished. Painting freely, building with loose parts, and sensory play with rice or water are all open-ended, unlike a worksheet or a step-by-step craft.

**Why is open-ended play good for my child?**
Because the child makes all the decisions, which builds attention, problem solving, creativity and confidence. Those are the exact strengths that hold their value as children grow, and they are grown far better through free play than through structured activities.

**How do I set up open-ended play at home?**
Offer good materials and then step back. A sheet of paper and a couple of paints, a tub of rice with cups and scoops, or a box of loose parts from your recycling with some tape. Keep it out and reachable, follow their lead, and let the mess happen.

**How long should a 3 to 5 year old play like this?**
As long as they stay interested, which is often longer than you expect once the pressure of a right answer is gone. Ten minutes is plenty on a busy day, and you can leave the setup out to come back to.
