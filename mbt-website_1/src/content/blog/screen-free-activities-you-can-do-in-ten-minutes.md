Some days you have the energy for finger painting and a craft table. Most days you have ten minutes and whatever is in the cupboard. Good news: ten minutes and what is in the cupboard is plenty. A short burst of real play does more for a small child than an hour of half-watched telly, and it costs nothing.

Here are twenty activities you can start in the next minute. Each one uses things you already have, and each one quietly grows one of the human strengths that matter most.

## Out in the world

1. **Watch the birds.** Sit somewhere with a view and simply notice who is out there. Count them. Copy their sounds. This grows attention the exact opposite way a screen shrinks it.
2. **Make a bird bath.** A shallow dish, a few stones, fresh water. Set it out and watch who visits over the coming days. We give birds water, never food.
3. **Chase your shadow.** On a sunny day, trace where their shadow falls, then check again a couple of hours later. Guess first, then look.
4. **Puddle science.** After rain, find a puddle and wonder together where it will go by tomorrow. Come back and see.
5. **A stick and a patch of dirt.** Draw, dig, build. It is the oldest toy there is, and one of the best.

## In the kitchen

6. **Pour and fill.** A few cups of different sizes, a jug, a towel underneath. Let them pour between cups and work out which holds the most. The spills are part of the lesson.
7. **Sink or float.** Fill a bowl, gather a few small objects, and have them guess before each one goes in.
8. **Sort the cutlery.** Spoons here, forks there. Simple sorting is early maths, and small children love a proper job.
9. **Smell test.** Hold out something from the pantry with their eyes closed and let them guess. Cinnamon, a lemon, a bit of coffee.
10. **Mix and watch.** A spoon of bicarb and a splash of vinegar in the sink. Wonder out loud at the fizz.

## On the move

11. **Freeze dance.** Hum a tune, stop suddenly, everyone freezes. Silly, active, and great for self-control.
12. **Animal walks.** Hop like a frog, waddle like a penguin, stomp like an elephant across the room.
13. **Balance the line.** A line of tape or a crack in the path becomes a tightrope.
14. **Fast and slow.** Move like a snail, then like a cheetah. Talk about the difference.
15. **Hide and seek, one room.** Even a tiny version delights a three year old and builds a little patience.

## Quiet ten minutes

16. **Build a nest.** Cushions and a blanket, then be birds together for a while.
17. **Draw what you saw today.** Not a lesson, just their own version of something from the day.
18. **Name a thing for every letter.** Animals, foods, whatever they like. A gentle game you can play anywhere.
19. **Cloud watching.** Lie back and find a cloud shaped like something. Wonder what it will do next.
20. **Feelings faces.** Pull a happy face, a cross face, a surprised face, and name each one. Naming a feeling is the first step to handling it.

## The only rule that matters

You do not need to run these perfectly. Follow their lead, hang back more than you think you should, and let the mess happen. Forget the tidy result. The point is ten minutes of your child deciding, trying, wondering and doing, in the real world, with you.
